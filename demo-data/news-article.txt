KARACHI PORT CUSTOMS STRIKE ENTERS FOURTH DAY — IMPORT CORRIDOR PARALYZED
Source: Pakistan Logistics Today (verified industry news)
Published: 2026-05-19, 07:14 PKT
Byline: Asad Hussain, Industrial Correspondent
URL: https://pakistan-logistics-today.example/news/2026-05-19/karachi-customs-strike

KARACHI — The customs officers' wildcat strike at Karachi Port Trust (KPT)
entered its fourth consecutive day on Tuesday, with industry bodies warning that
the prolonged stoppage has effectively paralyzed the country's primary import
corridor. Container clearance has slowed to a trickle, and an estimated
1,200+ trucks remain stalled inside and around the port perimeter.

A spokesperson for KPT confirmed that "the port remains operational on a 24/7
basis, and vessel berthing has not been suspended." However, freight forwarders
report that without customs clearance, containers cannot leave the bonded
zone — making the operational status of the berths "technically true but
practically irrelevant," in the words of one logistics manager.

The Pakistan Industrial Manufacturers' Association (PIMA) said in a statement
that consignments destined for Punjab-based distribution hubs, particularly
in Lahore and Faisalabad, are seeing delivery windows extended by 18 to 30 days
versus contracted lead times. Industrial chemicals, adhesives, polymer
intermediates, and packaging materials are reportedly the worst affected
categories.

Karachi Industrial Adhesives Ltd. (a primary supplier of SKU-1234 industrial
adhesives to several northern distribution hubs) has informed customers via
written notice that "all dispatches scheduled between 2026-05-13 and 2026-05-22
will be delayed by no fewer than 18 days, pending customs resolution." The
company's on-time delivery score has reportedly fallen below 70% in the
current month.

Operations directors at downstream distribution facilities have begun
activating backup supplier contracts and emergency procurement budgets.
The PIMA recommends that buyers with critical inventory floors approaching
breach should "trigger reserve clauses and qualified secondary supplier
agreements immediately, rather than wait for the strike to resolve."

There is no confirmed timeline for resolution as of press time. The Federal
Board of Revenue (FBR) has called an emergency meeting with striking officers
for Wednesday morning.
